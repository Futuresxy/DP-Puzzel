# DeepSeek V4/V4.1 co-design findings

**2026-11-13 · N12 DP evidence · analytical chip/GEMM results.** The study evaluated **2,196 candidates** from nine DP ratios, 27 physical profile/layout combinations and eight workload cases. The fixed-deployment sweep tested 24 precision plans per operating point and checked 414 paired migrations. The anchor uses batch 1 and 2,048-token prefill. V4-Flash uses the full prefill path and V4.1-Flash uses the simplified CED path. Their modeled scopes cover **97.16%** and **99.42%** of their respective ledger GEMM MACs. ## Power and energy

Use power for feasibility at the relevant electrical or thermal averaging window and compare energy per fixed job among feasible candidates. Report latency independently. Energy is the integral of power over the job, and average power is that energy divided by the same window. A sustained budget can change the feasible schedule even when average job power is low. Commercial controllers may reduce clocks under power pressure. Here the model holds 1 GHz and limits concurrent arrays. [NVIDIA clock-event documentation](https://docs.nvidia.com/deploy/nvidia-smi/index.html#clocks-event-reasons)

The DP area input is post-synthesis standard-cell area. Active power comes from sustained gate-level activity replay before CTS and routing. The largest DP reports **16.29 mW in BF16, 52.85 mW in MXFP8 and 38.91 mW in MXFP4**. Full issue specifies request rate. Mode selection and operand switching determine how much of the circuit toggles. Model inputs also determine tile tails, idle intervals and mode occupancy. The replay window used Qwen3-8B MLP activity, so the study uses a DP activity proxy. The power envelope is the modeled concurrent DP demand, and transient/board power and throttling behavior require separate characterization. [DP measurements](../../../../hw/data/dp_latest_area_power_20261112.md#逐精度功耗数据) · [CMOS dynamic-power model](https://web.ece.ucsb.edu/~parhami/pres_folder/f31-book-arith-pres-pt7.pdf)

## Findings

### 1. Local bandwidth can reverse the ranking from absolute compute capacity

Fix the native routed-FP4 plan and the 4×16×4 layout. In V4.1 the capacity-only lower bound favors **1:6:12** at **41.80 ms**, versus **50.91 ms** for **1:2:4**. After tiling and feedback, 1:6:12 retains a compute-only advantage (**43.58 vs 52.28 ms**). At **4 TB/s per SM** of local ingress, the ordering reverses to **106.59 ms for 1:2:4** and **122.61 ms for 1:6:12**. The latter spends about **79.0 ms** in modeled local traffic service versus **54.3 ms** for the baseline. Wider K raises bytes per issued tile, and the fixed area budget fits fewer cores with independent local ports. [Anchor data](figures/anchor_evidence.csv) · [Bandwidth-rank figure](figures/bandwidth_rank.pdf)

Coarse-to-fine probing finds a shared transition from **1:2:4 to 1:4:4 at 2.50–2.75 TB/s per SM** for the routed W4A8 path. Routed W4A4 delays the transition. V4 reaches **1:4:8 at 6.50–7.00 TB/s per SM**. V4.1 passes through **1:2:6 at 6.00–6.50**, then reaches **1:4:8 at 6.50–7.00**. These are sampled brackets under the stated mapping and port assumptions. The machine balance uses the compute and byte rates at the same local interface. For instance, at 4 TB/s the FP4 per-SM peak-to-read-bandwidth ratio is **4.096 MAC/byte** for 1:2:4 and **12.288 MAC/byte** for 1:6:12. In the rank figure, circles mark the latency winners and crosses mark the lowest DP energy. [Winner brackets](figures/bandwidth_boundaries.csv) · [Roofline model](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5tz795vq)

### 2. The lower-average-power design can need a larger power budget

Fix V4.1, routed W4A8, 4 TB/s per SM and the canonical layout. Uncapped **1:4:4** finishes in **131.26 ms** and averages **67.05 W** in the DP proxy. The **1:2:4** baseline takes **137.18 ms** and averages **71.22 W**. Their modeled DP envelopes are **213.55 W** and **147.47 W**. At a **150 W DP budget**, the concurrency-limited search selects **1:2:4**. At **250 W** it selects **1:4:4**. [Power and energy figure](figures/power_energy_tradeoff.pdf) · [Anchor data](figures/anchor_evidence.csv)

With routed W4A4 and the same 4 TB/s, **1:8:16** uses **5.5% less modeled DP energy** than the fastest **1:2:4** (**6.47 vs 6.85 J**), takes **25.4% longer** (**133.62 vs 106.59 ms**) and has a **297.67 W** envelope. The energy winner changes to **1:2:12** in the 10% idle-dynamic sensitivity for V4.1. The result supports keeping energy, time and power-envelope feasibility as separate outputs and measuring idle behavior before using small energy gaps to choose hardware. [Anchor data](figures/anchor_evidence.csv)

### 3. Migration order depends on the operator and the fixed hardware

On fixed **1:8:16**, V4-Flash and 4 TB/s per SM, Grouped O-down alone saves **55.45 ms** before conversion by reassigning **11.0%** of modeled MACs. Migrating all routed-expert GEMMs saves **47.01 ms** while moving **49.5%**. The Grouped O-down move changes BF16 to FP4 and relieves the scarce H-mode capacity on this profile. On the **1:2:4** baseline, the same Grouped O-down move saves **30.13 ms**, while the expert move saves **45.67 ms**, reversing the priority. The saved time is also the aggregate budget for extra serial conversion before that latency benefit disappears. The sweep assumes preformatted inputs and all these plans have unknown quality. [Fixed-hardware migration figure](figures/precision_migration.pdf) · [Anchor data](figures/anchor_evidence.csv)

### 4. Partitioning alone can change the preferred compute ratio

Hold the V4.1 routed-W4A8 workload, bandwidth, deployment and precision constant. On **1:4:4**, pooling independent output tiles across expert and projection groups finishes in **131.26 ms**. Isolating each weight group behind a launch barrier takes **143.18 ms** with the same useful and issued MACs. That ablation makes **1:2:4** the fastest layout at **141.64 ms**. The pooled policy preserves separate weights and keeps each output's K chain on one array. In a separate supply ablation, ideal B sharing across row groups moves the V4.1 routed-FP4 winner from **1:2:4** to **1:2:6** at the same nominal 4 TB/s. The size of this effect depends on whether the broadcast and context state can be realized. [Anchor data](figures/anchor_evidence.csv)

### 5. The most frequent winner can be a worse fixed choice

Across **30 matched routed-FP4 cases** on the canonical layout, **1:2:4** wins latency **18** times but can be **26.5%** slower than the per-case best. **1:2:8** wins only **twice** and limits the worst sampled latency gap to **10.8%**, the smallest of the nine. This is a useful fixed-choice criterion for a mixed service with an unknown operating mix. It is a finite-grid result over the sampled models, lengths, batches and bandwidths. ## Expand and validate

The current search groups repeated shapes without merging expert weights, caches native-K geometry and array service separately, and refines bandwidth intervals where coarse-grid winners differ. This reuses most of the work when adding a bandwidth, cap or precision point. The Pareto tables preserve area, latency, DP envelope and energy separately. Broader studies can enumerate legal discrete profiles and layouts first, sample continuous budget and traffic assumptions jointly, and spend further evaluations around Pareto and feasibility boundaries. Validate interactions such as bandwidth × broadcast, routing skew × batch and activity × power cap before discarding candidates based on a single-factor sweep. Report per-workload regret and results on held-out cases. A same-winner pair of grid endpoints can still conceal a crossover inside the interval.

| Validation tier | Data to obtain | Compare against the model |
|---|---|---|
| DP activity | BF16, MXFP8 and MXFP4 replays from real target operator traces at the same PVT/frequency. Include full issue, bubbles, stalls, idle and K tails, with an independent workload/design holdout. | Dynamic and leakage power, joules per issued request, sensitivity to operand distributions and enable patterns. |
| Array execution | Counter or RTL timestamps for Grouped O-down, routed gate/up/down with skewed per-expert M, and SWA/CSA QK and PV. Vary the native-K tail, context depth, layout, broadcast and input port pressure. | Issued requests, padded bytes, RAW/drain cycles and local service time. Calibrate against a subset and report median and p90 relative error on held-out shapes, profiles and contexts. |
| Chip and quality | Multi-SM power-window and clock telemetry, on-chip/DRAM counters, real per-expert and key-block traces, plus external full-plan quantization evaluations. | Measured ranking and crossover intervals, clock response to power limits, local vs HBM traffic, and latency/energy only for plans that pass the quality constraint. |

The current run passed hand-computable schedule and power tests plus full-grid checks for useful/issued work, time components, energy consistency, fixed-deployment migration and power-cap accounting. The DP area and active-mode power inputs are measurement-backed. Array service, chip timing and DeepSeek activity remain uncalibrated. The relevant validation target is the measured gap between alternatives, especially near a selection boundary.
